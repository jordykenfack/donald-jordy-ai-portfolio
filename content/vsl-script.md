websites, automation and AI"*). Two cuts below: a **short version** sized to the current
2:14 placeholder, and a **long version** for a standalone sales asset (e.g. a dedicated

cost, technical skill to maintain it, fear of picking the wrong agency/platform, and
uncertainty over ROI — and reuse the site's own positioning line: *"A website should be
the beginning of a system — not the end."*

Grounding stats used below (real, not invented):

## LONG VERSION (~7–9 min)

### Opening Hook

Somewhere between a quarter and 40% of small businesses still don't have a website.
That number's been shrinking for years — which sounds like good news, until you look at
the businesses that do have one.

Because having a website isn't the same as having a website that works. Most of them are
digital business cards. Someone lands on them, looks around for fifteen seconds, and
leaves — and the business owner has no idea whether that visitor was ever going to buy,
or whether the site had anything to do with it either way.

If you're a business owner who's thought about getting a new site, or fixing the one you
have, I'd bet you've run into one of three walls. The cost felt bigger than the payoff.
You don't have anyone in-house who can touch the thing once it's live. Or you already
tried once — picked an agency or a platform that seemed right — and ended up with
something that looks fine and changes nothing about how many customers you get.

None of those are really about "getting a website." They're about not knowing whether
the money you spend is going to come back to you. And that uncertainty is the actual
problem — not your industry, not your competitors, not some algorithm. In this video I
want to walk you through why that happens, and the different way I approach it that
fixes it.

### Mistake #1 — Treating the website like a cost, not infrastructure

Mistake number one I see constantly: a website gets budgeted like an expense instead of
built like infrastructure. So the instinct is to spend as little as possible — a cheap
template, a marketplace freelancer, a builder you drag and drop yourself. On paper that
seems smart. Why spend real money on something you could get for a fraction of the price?

Except a cheap template with no strategy behind it doesn't capture a single lead more
than having no website at all. You saved money on the build and lost it forever on every
visitor who showed up and left without you ever knowing they existed. Cheap isn't cheap
if it converts at zero.

### Mistake #2 — Picking based on portfolio, not process

Mistake number two: choosing who builds it based on how their portfolio looks, not how
they think. You scroll a few sites, find one you like the aesthetic of, and assume the
process behind it will translate to your business. But a beautiful site for a lifestyle
brand and a lead-generation site for a B2B logistics company are solving completely
different problems. If the person you hire doesn't ask about your customer, your sales
process, and what happens after someone fills out a form, you're not hiring a strategist —
you're hiring a decorator. And decorators don't move your business forward, they just
move pixels.

This is exactly why "picking the wrong agency" is such a real fear. It's not really about
taste. It's about whether the person you hire is thinking about your business, or just
their next portfolio piece.

### Mistake #3 — Believing a good-looking site is the finish line

Mistake number three, and this is the big one: believing that once the site looks good,
the job is done. You launch it, it looks sharp, you're proud of it — and then nothing
changes. No new leads. No fewer manual follow-ups. Same amount of work running your
business as before, just now with a nicer-looking website attached to it.

That's because a website was never supposed to be the finish line. It's the beginning of
a system — not the end of one.

### The Real Problem

Here's the pattern in all three mistakes: they all treat "getting a website" as a
one-time purchase instead of an ongoing system that has to actually do something —
capture the right visitor, move their information somewhere useful, and follow up
without you having to remember to. When that system isn't there, it doesn't matter how
the site looks. It's a brochure sitting in a drawer nobody opens.

And that's the real reason most business owners feel so uncertain about ROI. It's not
that websites don't work. It's that most of the ones built for small businesses were
never built to be measured in the first place.

### Introducing the Approach

I'm Donald Jordy. I build websites — but that's really the entry point. What I actually
build is the system behind the website: the part that turns a visitor into a lead, a
lead into a qualified conversation, and a qualified conversation into a booked call,
without you manually chasing any of it.

Traffic comes in. It hits a website built to convert, not just to look nice. That becomes
a lead. The lead flows into a CRM. AI qualifies it, figuring out who's serious and who's
just browsing. Serious leads get followed up automatically. And what lands on your
calendar is a booked call — not a stranger's email sitting unread for four days.

That's the difference between a website and a system. One sits there. The other works
while you sleep.

### The 3 Phases

I approach every project across three phases.

**Phase one — clarity before design.** Before I open a design tool, I need to know what
result you actually need: more leads, more bookings, fewer manual follow-ups, a cleaner
brand for reaching bigger clients. Every design and automation decision after this point
gets judged against that one answer, not against what looks trendy this year.

**Phase two — build the system, not just the surface.** This is where the site gets
built, around the action you want someone to take, with the lead capture, CRM
connections, and AI qualification wired in from day one — not bolted on six months later
when you realize the pretty site isn't bringing in customers.

**Phase three — make it yours to run.** This is the part most agencies skip, and it's
the one that solves the "I don't have the technical skills to update it" fear directly.
You shouldn't need to call me every time you want to change a headline or add a project
to your portfolio. I build on tools you can actually use yourself, while the automation
underneath keeps running without you touching it at all.

### What You Get

In practice, that means I work with businesses across three types of projects.
**Websites** — landing pages, business sites, personal brands, custom builds — designed
around the action you want visitors to take, not just built to look good in a screenshot.
**AI automation** — the lead workflows, follow-ups, CRM processes and onboarding that
shouldn't require a human doing them by hand. And **custom digital systems**, for when
the answer isn't "a website" or "an automation" but something that connects your data,
your workflows and your website into one thing that actually works together.

### Proof

I've built this for a personal brand positioning an AI engineer's services with automated
lead capture built in from the start. For a freight company running routes between Turkey
and Ghana, where the site needed to turn a browsing visitor into an instant quote
request, not just a phone number nobody calls. For a trade platform matching Turkish
exporters with African buyers across entire product categories. For a business summit
that needed the site to convert into actual seat reservations, not just page views.

Different industries, different goals — same underlying approach: figure out the result
you actually need first, then build everything backward from there.

### Addressing the Objections Directly

I want to speak straight to the three things probably going through your head right now.

If the cost is the concern — a website built as real infrastructure isn't priced against
a bargain-bin template, it's priced against what one converted lead is worth to your
business over the next year. A site like this typically pays for itself several times
over in its first year, because it's built to be measured, not just admired.

If it's the fear of being locked into something you can't touch — you won't be. You'll
be able to update your own site. And if you ever want to work with someone else down the
line, nothing here is built to trap you.

And if it's simply not knowing whether any of this will bring you actual customers —
that's the entire reason the system exists. Not a website you hope works, but one built
with the lead capture, the follow-up, and the qualification already inside it, so you're
not finding out six months from now whether it worked.
